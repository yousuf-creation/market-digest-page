Anthropic shipped a cheap new model, and ten people wrote about Claude for ten different reasons

Claude was the loudest word this week, but it was not one story. The real news item is Claude Haiku 5.5, a small, fast, cheap model. Simon Willison and Swyx both have the same numbers on it: $0.10 per million words in and $0.50 out, as long as your prompt stays under 100,000 tokens. Above that it jumps five times, to $0.50 and $2.50. Both say that under 100,000 tokens it matches OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna on price and beats it on test scores, and that above that line Luna is the better buy. Willison adds a catch Swyx does not: the new model counts words differently, so the same long prompt uses about 1.25x more tokens than before — a quiet price rise. Swyx adds a catch Willison does not: at full effort it writes about 162,000 words of output per task, roughly three times Luna's 50,000. Matt Wolfe puts a plain number on it — about 21 cents per task on average, about a quarter of Sonnet's cost. Julian Goldie has the benchmark jumps (72.4% on OSWorld 2.1, up from 15.7%) and says Anthropic itself still points people to Sonnet and Opus for hard work.

The other eight are about using Claude, not about the model. Duncan Rogoff covers how to write Claude skills so Claude actually picks them. Ryan Doser argues you should take someone else's skill file and rework it. Jono Catliff shows a free Claude agent that runs a Google Ads account. Jack Roberts has three separate videos — Adobe inside Claude, free UI animations, and design hacks — plus one that is really a Bluehost advert. Nate Herk has two: a pick of GitHub repos, and running Claude Code inside a Grok bot. Julian Goldie has a second one claiming an SEO system took a site from 0 to 1,300 clicks a day. Peter Yang's piece is about agent workflow generally, and part of it sits behind a paywall. And The Rundown AI's Claude mention is a rule change: from 12 November, Anthropic will bar "sustained and needless" abuse of Claude.

Why Claude ignores your skills

Duncan Rogoff says Anthropic updated its guide to building Claude skills, and a skill's description decides whether Claude uses it.

  • Anthropic's biggest rule is to keep each skill under 500 lines.
  • Claude can pick from over 100 skills, but at first it only sees each name and description.

  • A skill starts as one plain text file, so no code is needed.
  • Anthropic says to build three tests before writing the skill. Run Claude without the skill first. Then write only enough to fix what broke.

Agentic engineering workflow ras mic

Peter Yang shares a podcast episode where engineer Micky Shimeles explains a 4-step way to ship real apps with AI agents without making sloppy work.

📂 Persuasion mental models for marketing

Corey Haines says marketing is mostly psychology, and two ideas, loss aversion and social proof, can help get people to say yes.

  • Loss aversion: people feel a loss more than a gain of the same size. Corey shares a story about losing a cheap guitar at age 16 and feeling worse than the joy of getting a nicer one.
  • To use loss aversion: lead with what people lose by not buying, be generous upfront, use lazy registration so people keep their progress, use FOMO (fear of missing out), and pre-fill carts with add-ons.
  • Social proof: people copy what others do, most of all when they are unsure. Corey cites Robert Cialdini's book, Influence.
  • To use social proof: show testimonials, share counts, subscriber or customer numbers, endorsements, media mentions, case studies and user activity.
  • Corey says an audit of all your marketing for these ideas can make it "doubly as effective."

"When you say it, it’s marketing. When your customer says it, it’s social proof."
