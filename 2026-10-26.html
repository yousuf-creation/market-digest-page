Three new studies say AI assistants send almost no traffic

Rand Fishkin and Amanda Natividad both published on the same question this week, and both reach the same answer. Al Iverson's updated Gmail guide: Google now blocks, it does not just filter

Iverson rewrote his Gmail deliverability guide this week. The headline point is that Gmail now outright blocks mail that fails its technical rules — duplicate headers and broken DNS among them. In the 2025 holiday period, broken or missing PTR records caused the most Gmail rejections, tens of millions of messages in a few weeks. Google Postmaster Tools now flags any spam rate above 0.1%. His sending advice: SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up, no bought or rented lists, and mail only people who opened or clicked in the past 9 to 12 months for at least 30 days. After a deferral, stop for 10 to 15 minutes, stay under the limit for 24 hours, then ramp about 25% a day. He also says to drop fake "Re:" prefixes, emoji checkmarks in display names, and code tricks that claim to force the Primary inbox.

Anthropic's Claude Mods let you rebuild the Claude Code interface in code

Writing in Swyx's newsletter, Dan McAteer explains Claude Mods: plugins written in TypeScript or JavaScript that react to events like a prompt or a tool call and change what you see. Claude can write a mod from a plain-language request. Anthropic ships six built in — one, You Should Know, runs a side agent that warned about 45 other files holding unfinished work before edits were thrown away. McAteer's argument is about control: ChatGPT's Intelligent UI lets the model pick the interface, Cursor's PR Routing lets teams set review rules, and Mods put it in your hands. The costs are real too. McAteer's own Next Steps Supervisor fired on 99 of 111 turns since 15 October, each check costing roughly 5–6k input-equivalent tokens on a 50k-token session. Mods are not sandboxed.
