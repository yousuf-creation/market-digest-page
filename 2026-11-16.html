How to fix ai slop with claude design nate parrott

Peter Yang shares an episode where Nate Parrott, the Anthropic designer behind Claude Design, gives 5 tips for getting Claude to design without AI slop.

Ethan Mollick tested Claude Fable 5.2 on many hard tasks and says it is "almost" AGI, because it does expert-level work but cannot tell when its own work is not good enough.

  • Fable did expert-level work as a filmmaker, hardware hacker, business planner, literary scholar, researcher and 3D world builder. Each was done from a short prompt.

"Knowing when your own work is not good enough is a large part of what makes someone an expert, and it is the part Fable does least reliably."

Microsoft bounced mail from domains with no DMARC, then had an outage

Al Iverson reports two separate Microsoft email problems in one week. From 6 to 9 November, Microsoft's consumer mailboxes — Outlook.com and Hotmail — bounced mail from domains with no DMARC record, even when SPF and DKIM passed. Business Microsoft 365 was not affected. Iverson says it looked unintended and was rolled back by 9 November. Then from 12 to 13 November a separate outage hit both consumer and business mail, with people locked out of webmail and mail delayed. Microsoft rolled back a network change and mail recovered by 13 November. Iverson says any domain that sends email needs DMARC, even a small sender, and that it is one DNS record. He also says to check sending reports for a spike in suppressed Outlook, Hotmail or Microsoft 365 addresses on 12-13 November, and to ask the sending platform to reactivate them.

Alison Gootee's piece lands near this. Gootee writes about BIMI, the tool that shows a brand's logo next to its emails in Gmail, Yahoo and Apple Mail. To get it, a sender has to set DMARC to enforcement. Gootee cites Darktrace seeing a 620% lift in Black Friday phishing, Sinch research where 53% of people got a real brand message that felt suspicious, and a survey of 1,500 US consumers where 88% said a verified badge would raise trust. Only 5.7% of senders have BIMI. So two different people this week pointed at DMARC for two different reasons.

Meta is adding discounts to ads that advertisers did not approve

Common Thread Collective reports that Meta has a default setting called "Detected offers" which pulls discounts off a brand's website and puts them into Facebook and Instagram ads — including on ads where promotions were switched off.
